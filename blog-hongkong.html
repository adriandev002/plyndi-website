Hong Kong in 3 Days: Dim Sum, the Peak & the Star Ferry

By Adrian Updated September 15, 2026 10 min read

Hong Kong packs an enormous amount into a small footprint — you can go from a Buddhist monastery to a rooftop bar to a wet market in the space of an afternoon, all connected by one of the most efficient transit systems anywhere.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
October–December (cooler, drier, clearer skies). Avoid the hot, humid, typhoon-prone summer months if you can.
Getting around
Octopus card works on MTR, buses, trams, and the Star Ferry — get one on arrival, usable for small purchases too.
Minimum time needed
3 full days for the highlights.
Rough daily budget
$40–60/day budget, $90–150/day mid-range — accommodation is the main expense driver here.

1. Victoria Peak without the markup

The Peak Tram up Victoria Peak is iconic but the ticket lines can run over an hour without advance booking — buy tickets online ahead of time. A genuinely free alternative with a comparable (some would say better) view: the free public viewing terraces partway up, or take bus 15 up instead of the tram and walk the Peak Circle Walk once there for a quieter, less commercialized experience than the main viewing platform.

2. Dim sum: what's actually worth the wait

Hong Kong invented modern dim sum culture, and the difference between a good and mediocre spot is real. Skip hotel dim sum brunches aimed at tourists and look for:

  • Tim Ho Wan — the world's cheapest Michelin-starred restaurant, multiple branches, genuinely excellent and priced like a regular meal.
  • Older, cart-service dim sum halls in Sham Shui Po or Mong Kok — where servers push carts between tables rather than order-by-checklist, an increasingly rare and worthwhile experience.

Order har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai, char siu bao, and cheung fun as a baseline, then branch out from there.

Pro tip Go for dim sum before 11am on weekends if you want to avoid the longest waits — it's a genuine weekend ritual for many Hong Kong families, and the best spots fill up fast.

3. The Star Ferry: still worth it

The Star Ferry between Hong Kong Island and Kowloon costs a small fraction of a dollar and has been running since 1888 — it's one of the cheapest, most scenic things to do in the city, giving you a harbor-level view of the skyline that the MTR (which also crosses the harbor, underground) can't match. Take it at least once, ideally around sunset.

4. Wet markets and old neighborhoods

For a side of Hong Kong that isn't skyscrapers, walk through a wet market — Sham Shui Po for electronics and fabric bargains alongside the food market, or Graham Street Market on Hong Kong Island, one of the oldest surviving markets in the city, squeezed between modern high-rises.

5. Temples and a break from the density

Man Mo Temple, filled with enormous spiral incense coils burning overhead, is a short, atmospheric visit in the middle of the city. For actual green space and quiet, the Big Buddha and Po Lin Monastery on Lantau Island (reached by cable car or bus) is a genuine half-day trip and a complete change of pace from the density of Kowloon and Hong Kong Island.

6. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Not booking Peak Tram tickets in advance — the walk-up line is one of the longest in the city on a clear day.
  • Underestimating how expensive hotels are relative to food and transit — budget disproportionately more for accommodation than in most Southeast Asian stops.
  • Skipping the outlying islands (Lantau, Cheung Chau) — Hong Kong is far more than the dense urban core, and the islands offer a completely different pace.
  • Not getting an Octopus card immediately — it works everywhere, including convenience stores, and saves constant single-ticket purchases.
