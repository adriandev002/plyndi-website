Pingtung & Kenting: Beaches, Taiwan's Southern Tip & Xiaoliuqiu

By Adrian Updated October 8, 2026 3 min read

Pingtung is Taiwan's tropical south. Kenting National Park has the island's best-known beaches and its southernmost tip, and a short ferry ride away is Xiaoliuqiu, a small coral island where you can swim near wild sea turtles. It's a beach trip with a bit of ocean wind.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
November–April is dry and mild but windy, with strong northeast monsoon winds from roughly October to March. Summer is beach season but hot, with typhoon risk from July to October.
Getting there
HSR to Zuoying (Kaohsiung), then the Kenting Express bus, which takes about 2.5–3 hours; check current times. Pingtung city is reachable by regular train.
Getting around
Scooters are the local default, but you need a valid license. Otherwise use taxis, bicycles and the local tourist shuttle buses around Hengchun and Kenting Street.
Minimum time needed
2 nights in Kenting, 3 if you add Xiaoliuqiu.
Rough daily budget
$35–55/day budget, $70–120/day mid-range. Prices spike on weekends and holidays, so a weekday trip is far cheaper. These are estimates, so check current rates.

1. Kenting basics

Kenting National Park was Taiwan's first national park, and the area around Kenting Street is the busy holiday strip, with shops, food stalls and a night market. If you prefer quieter evenings, base yourself in Hengchun Old Town, a short ride away, which still has its Qing-era city walls from the 1870s and local noodle shops that aren't aimed at tourists.

2. Beaches and the southern tip

  • Baisha Beach — pale sand and calmer water, a good first stop.
  • Nanwan / Little Bay — the busiest beaches, with water sports rentals, and fun for people-watching.
  • Eluanbi Lighthouse and Park — a lighthouse from the 1880s at the southern end of the island, with a short coastal trail.
  • Fengchuisha — windswept dunes and a very good place to watch the sunset.

Pay attention to flags and lifeguard signs. Several Kenting beaches have rip currents, and swimming outside marked zones is a real risk.

Pro tip Go on a weekday. Kenting is a different place Monday to Thursday, with lower prices, empty beaches and no queues at the ferry. Weekends and national holidays can cost double and feel crowded.

3. Xiaoliuqiu: the turtle island

Xiaoliuqiu is reached by a short ferry (about 25–30 minutes) from Donggang. You can circle the island by scooter or bike in a few hours, and snorkeling spots near the coast often have green sea turtles. Keep your distance: touching or chasing turtles is against the rules and can lead to fines. In summer, book your ferry and accommodation early.

4. Donggang and seafood

Donggang is the departure point for the island and a serious seafood town. The bluefin tuna season, usually in spring, draws crowds for sashimi and festival events. Eat here before or after the ferry rather than on the island, where prices are higher.

5. Beyond the beach

Inland Pingtung has a strong Indigenous heritage. Sandimen is home to Paiwan and Rukai communities and the Taiwan Indigenous Peoples Cultural Park, which makes a worthwhile half-day if you have a car.

6. A sample 3-day plan

  • Day 1: HSR to Zuoying, then the Kenting Express bus. Check in, then a sunset at Fengchuisha or Baisha Beach and a Kenting Street dinner.
  • Day 2: Eluanbi Lighthouse in the morning, Nanwan or Little Bay for water sports, and a Hengchun Old Town dinner.
  • Day 3: Bus or taxi to Donggang for seafood, then the ferry to Xiaoliuqiu. Snorkel with the turtles (from a distance), and stay overnight or return.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Underestimating the wind — in winter it's strong enough to ruin a beach day, so check the forecast and bring a windbreaker.
  • Swimming outside marked areas — rip currents are a real hazard on several beaches.
  • Renting a scooter without a license — rental shops are casual, but insurance and the law aren't.
  • Booking Xiaoliuqiu at the last minute — in peak season ferries and rooms fill up.
