Da Nang for First-Timers: Dragon Bridge, Beaches & Day Trips

By Adrian Updated October 9, 2026 3 min read

Da Nang is a beach city with a real city attached: a long, clean coastline, a river-front with a fire-breathing bridge, cheap seafood, and the mountains and old towns of central Vietnam within an easy day trip. It suits first-timers who want the sea and the sights without the chaos of Hanoi or Saigon.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
February–May for dry, warm beach weather. Roughly September–December is the wet and typhoon-prone season, so expect rain and rough seas.
Getting there
Da Nang International Airport (DAD) is close to the center and has domestic flights from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City plus international routes. Trains on the Hanoi–Saigon line also stop in the city.
Getting around
Grab and taxis are cheap and easy. The beach, river and center are close enough to cover by short rides; rent a bike only if you are confident in Vietnamese traffic.
Minimum time needed
2 days for the city and beach, 3–4 if you add Hoi An, Ba Na Hills or the Hai Van Pass.
Rough daily budget
About $25–40/day on a budget, $60–90/day mid-range, not counting flights. These are estimates, so check current prices before you book.

1. Where to base yourself

Da Nang is long and thin, so where you sleep matters more than which hotel you pick.

  • My Khe / An Thuong area — the main beach strip, with cafes, seafood restaurants and plenty of mid-range hotels. The best choice if you want sea in the morning and food within walking distance.
  • Han River / city center — closer to the Dragon Bridge, the Cham Museum and local restaurants, and a short ride to the beach.
  • Son Tra Peninsula — quiet, green and mostly resorts. Lovely if you want to switch off, less convenient if you want to wander at night.

2. The Dragon Bridge fire show

The Dragon Bridge breathes fire and water at 9pm on Saturday and Sunday nights, plus major public holidays. The show lasts about 10–12 minutes, is free, and has no tickets or official seating. Arrive around 8:15pm if you want a spot on the east-bank promenade, or watch from a cafe balcony on the opposite side for a calmer view.

Heavy rain or wind can cancel it at short notice, and on weeknights the bridge is only lit up, so plan your weekend nights around it and ask your hotel before you go.

Pro tip Do the Dragon Bridge show on a Saturday or Sunday, eat dinner early, and watch from a second-floor cafe on the west bank. You get the view without fighting the crowd on the promenade.

3. Beaches and the coast

My Khe is the city beach and the easiest place to swim and watch the sun come up. Go early: it is cooler, quieter, and the water is calmer in the morning. If it is the wet season, check the red-flag warnings before swimming, because currents can be strong.

For a change of scene, head to the Son Tra Peninsula for viewpoints and the Linh Ung Pagoda with its giant Lady Buddha statue, which overlooks the sea.

4. Marble Mountains and Ba Na Hills

The Marble Mountains are five limestone hills with caves, pagodas and viewpoints, about 20–30 minutes south of the center. Entry to Thuy Son Peak was listed at 40,000 VND in 2026. Go in the morning, wear shoes with grip, and expect stairs.

Ba Na Hills is the theme park with the Golden Bridge held by giant stone hands. In 2026 the adult ticket for foreign visitors was listed around 1,000,000 VND, so it is a big-ticket day. Arrive when it opens, because it fills with tour buses by late morning, and treat it as a half-day rather than a full one.

5. Add Hoi An and the Hai Van Pass

Hoi An's old town is about 30 km south and an easy day trip by Grab (roughly 250,000–350,000 VND each way as a 2026 estimate), or a stop on a motorbike tour over the Hai Van Pass. If you can spare the nights, base in Hoi An for one or two of them and read our Hoi An guide.

6. What to eat

  • Mi Quang — turmeric noodles with pork, shrimp and peanuts, the signature dish of the region.
  • Banh xeo and nem lui — crispy pancakes and lemongrass skewers, wrapped in herbs and rice paper.
  • Seafood along the beach — pick from the tanks and agree the price per kilo before they cook it.

7. A sample 3-day plan

  • Day 1: Beach morning at My Khe, a mi quang lunch, the Cham Museum or a cafe afternoon, then dinner near the river and the Dragon Bridge (on a weekend night).
  • Day 2: Marble Mountains in the morning, Linh Ung Pagoda and Son Tra viewpoints, seafood dinner.
  • Day 3: Ba Na Hills early, or swap it for a day trip to Hoi An's old town and An Bang beach.

8. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Arriving on a weeknight for the fire show — it only runs on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays.
  • Going to Ba Na Hills late — queues and crowds build quickly after about 10am.
  • Visiting in the wet season without a plan B — October to December rain can cancel beach days and the show.
  • Treating Da Nang as only a transit city for Hoi An — it has its own beach, food and bridge worth at least a night or two.
