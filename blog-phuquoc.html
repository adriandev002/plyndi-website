Phu Quoc for First-Timers: Beaches, Seasons, Visa & Getting Around

By Adrian Updated October 9, 2026 3 min read

Phu Quoc is Vietnam's largest island and its easiest beach holiday: white-sand beaches, sunsets on the west coast, seafood, and a new airport that makes it simple to reach. The catch is that it is spread out and the weather decides which side of the island is good.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
November–April is the dry season, with December–March generally best for beaches and boat trips. May–October is wetter, quieter and cheaper; from about June to September the east coast is more sheltered.
Getting there
Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) is about 10 km from Duong Dong, with flights from Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and some international cities. Ferries run from Rach Gia (about 2.5–3 hours) and Ha Tien (about 1–1.5 hours).
Getting around
There is no proper public transport. Taxis, Grab and hotel transfers work for most visitors; scooters rent for about 120,000–250,000 VND a day.
Minimum time needed
3 full days for beaches and one island day, 4–5 if you want a slow pace.
Rough daily budget
About $30–50/day on a budget, $70–120/day mid-range, not counting flights. Resort and attraction prices can push this much higher.

1. Visa-free stay on the island

Phu Quoc has a special rule allowing many foreign visitors to stay up to 30 days without a visa, but only under certain conditions, such as arriving by an international flight and staying on the island. Arriving by ferry from the mainland does not qualify, and the rules can change, so confirm the current conditions for your passport before booking.

2. Where to base yourself

  • Duong Dong — the main town, with the night market, ATMs and the port. Practical, less scenic.
  • Long Beach — about 20 km of west-coast sand where most hotels sit. The water can turn murky in the rainy season.
  • Ong Lang — a calmer beach about 7–8 km north-west of town with clear water even during the rains.
  • Sao Beach — white sand on the south-east coast, about 25 km from Duong Dong and often the most scenic. Better as a day trip than a base.
Pro tip If you are visiting between May and October, stay on or near Ong Lang for clearer water and use the east coast for beach days. In the dry season, choose a west-coast hotel for sunsets.

3. Getting around safely

A taxi or Grab from the airport costs roughly 120,000–300,000 VND, and VinBus also serves the airport. If you rent a scooter, you legally need an International Driving Permit with a motorcycle category as well as your home license, and many roads are unlit or unpaved, so avoid riding at night or in rain. Many travelers simply book a driver for a day at a time.

4. The big attractions

  • Hon Thom cable car — a long over-sea ride; roughly 850,000 VND round trip for adults.
  • VinWonders and Vinpearl Safari — each around 950,000 VND for adults, aimed at families.
  • Sunset Town and the Kiss Bridge — a Mediterranean-style district at the southern tip, best around sunset.
  • Night market in Duong Dong — seafood and snacks, more local than resort.

5. Islands, snorkeling and beaches by season

The calm dry season is the time for island hopping and snorkeling. In the wetter months some boat tours are cancelled because of rough seas. Pick your beach by season: west coast (Long Beach, Ong Lang) in the dry months, and the east coast (Sao Beach and neighbors) in the middle of the year.

6. What to eat

  • Fresh seafood — grilled squid, crab and prawns at the night market and beach restaurants.
  • Goi ca trich — herring salad, a Phu Quoc specialty.
  • Fish sauce — the island's most famous product; the local variety is a popular souvenir.

7. A sample 4-day plan

  • Day 1: Arrive, settle on the west coast, sunset at the beach, dinner at the Duong Dong night market.
  • Day 2: Island-hopping or snorkeling day trip if seas are calm, early dinner and rest.
  • Day 3: Sao Beach in the morning, then Sunset Town or the Hon Thom cable car in the afternoon.
  • Day 4: Long Beach or Ong Lang beach morning, then a fish-sauce or pepper-farm stop on the way to the airport.

8. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Booking one hotel without checking the season — the best side of the island changes during the year.
  • Assuming you can walk everywhere — the island is large, so plan transport.
  • Renting a scooter without the right license — insurance may not cover you, and roads are rough at night.
  • Visiting by ferry and expecting visa-free entry — the special exemption has conditions.
