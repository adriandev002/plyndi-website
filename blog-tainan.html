Tainan for First-Timers: Taiwan's Old Capital, Street Food & Anping

By Adrian Updated October 8, 2026 3 min read

Tainan is Taiwan's oldest city and, if you ask most Taiwanese people, its best food city, a claim they'll happily argue about for an hour. Walking it feels different from Taipei: temples on residential corners, fort walls, old shophouses, and breakfast that starts early.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
November–March for cooler, drier weather. Summers are very hot and humid, with typhoon risk from roughly July to October.
Getting there
HSR to Tainan station takes about 1 hour 45 minutes from Taipei, then a short train shuttle into Tainan Station. A regular express train takes around 4.5 hours.
Getting around
Walking plus bicycles, taxis and ride-hailing, and tourist shuttle buses (the 88 Anping line and 99 Old Capital line). Scooters are common but need the correct license.
Minimum time needed
2 days. Three if you want an unhurried Anping afternoon and a day outside the center.
Rough daily budget
$25–40/day budget, $50–80/day mid-range. Most street dishes are in the NT$50–150 range, so verify current prices on arrival.

1. Where to base yourself

Stay near Tainan Station or around Shennong Street / Guohua Street. Both put you within walking distance of the old-city sights and a lot of the best food, and the shuttle buses to Anping leave from the station area. You don't need a car.

2. Eating: the real reason to go

Tainan portions are deliberately small so you can eat in several places in a day. Plan four or five stops rather than two big meals.

  • Beef soup — fresh beef slices cooked in a ladle of hot broth, and a classic breakfast. It appears early in the morning and some famous places sell out by mid-morning.
  • Danzai noodles — a small bowl of noodles with shrimp broth and minced pork; Du Xiao Yue (established in 1895) is the well-known name.
  • Milkfish congee — a mild, comforting local breakfast.
  • Coffin bread — thick fried toast hollowed out and filled with a creamy stew, more fun than refined.
  • Shrimp rolls — fried rolls typically eaten around Anping.

Many well-known places close one day a week and some close early when they sell out. Check hours the day before.

Pro tip Eat in portions. Tainan snacks are small, so pace yourself across the day with four or five short stops rather than two big meals. Start before 8am if you want the famous breakfast places at their best.

3. The old city

Chihkan Tower (Fort Provintia) dates back to the Dutch period in the 1650s and sits in the middle of town. Nearby, the Confucius Temple is often described as Taiwan's first school and is quiet and photogenic early in the day. Shennong Street is an old lane lined with cafes, galleries and lanterns that looks best in the evening. The Hayashi Department Store, from the 1930s, is a Japanese-era building worth a look inside and a rooftop stop.

4. Anping half-day

Take the tourist shuttle bus from Tainan Station to Anping, where the Dutch built Fort Zeelandia in the 1620s. Walk the Anping Old Street for snacks, then see the Anping Tree House, an old warehouse swallowed by banyan roots. Late afternoon is best: the light is better and the heat is less punishing.

5. A day beyond the center

If you have an extra day, the Chimei Museum has an unusual collection of art, instruments and historical objects in a European-style building; check which day it closes before you go. Otherwise, use the extra day for a slow second round of eating and wandering.

6. A sample 2-day plan

  • Day 1: Early beef-soup breakfast, then Chihkan Tower, the Confucius Temple and Shennong Street. Snack through the afternoon, then Hayashi Department Store at golden hour and dinner nearby.
  • Day 2: Shuttle to Anping around mid-morning for Fort Zeelandia, the Old Street and the Tree House. Shrimp rolls for a snack, then back for a last round of danzai noodles before your train.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Arriving on the wrong day — many museums and some famous food shops close one day a week, often Monday.
  • Only going to famous shops — the line-free place next door is frequently just as good.
  • Walking the old city at noon in summer — go early, rest in the middle of the day and head out again after 4pm.
  • Renting a scooter without the right license — if you aren't licensed to ride, use buses, bicycles and taxis instead.
