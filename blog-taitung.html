Taitung for First-Timers: Rice Fields, Hot Springs & the Slow East Coast

By Adrian Updated October 8, 2026 3 min read

Taitung is the quiet side of Taiwan: mountains to the west, the Pacific to the east, wide rice fields between them, and a pace that makes Taipei feel hectic. It's less polished and harder to reach without wheels, which is exactly why it's worth the extra travel time.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
April–June and October–November are the most comfortable. Rice-harvest scenery is best around late June–July and October–November, and the hot-air balloon season at Luye Highland usually runs through summer, so check the current dates. Typhoons mostly hit July–October.
Getting there
Fast trains from Taipei (Puyuma or Taroko Express) take roughly 3.5–4 hours. There are also short domestic flights from Taipei, so check current schedules.
Getting around
A rental car or scooter is strongly recommended. Public buses exist but are infrequent, and distances between sights are long.
Minimum time needed
3 days. Combine with Hualien by train, which takes roughly two hours on a fast service.
Rough daily budget
$35–60/day plus car or scooter rental. Check current rental prices, as they vary by season.

1. Chishang and the rift valley

Chishang is the rice town of the East Rift Valley. Rent a bicycle at the station and ride out into the paddies; the tree-lined road made famous by a coffee commercial is known locally as Mr. Brown Avenue. Try the Chishang railway bento in its metal box: simple, cheap and made with locally grown rice. Go in the harvest months if the scenery matters most to you.

2. Luye Highland and the balloons

The Luye Highland area is known for its hot-air balloon festival, normally in summer, plus tea fields and rolling green hills. Tethered rides are usually offered in the early morning when the air is calm. During festival weeks accommodation fills up, so book ahead.

Pro tip Book the car before you book the hotels. Rental cars in Taitung sell out during the summer balloon season and on holidays, and without one you'll spend your trip waiting for buses.

3. The east coast

Driving the Pacific coast is the main event. Stop at Sanxiantai, an island reached by a long arched footbridge. The fishing harbor at Chenggong is good for fresh seafood, and Dulan is an old sugar-factory area that has become an arts and surf village with live music in the evenings. Leave a gap in the schedule: the point of the east coast is to stop where you like.

4. Zhiben hot springs

Zhiben is a hot-spring valley a short drive from Taitung city. Most baths are inside hotels, many with open-air pools, and the water is generally clear rather than the sulfur-heavy water of Beitou. It's an easy way to end a long driving day.

5. Taitung city and Indigenous culture

In the city, walk the Railway Art Village and the adjacent Tiehua Village, a market and live-music area built around the old station; opening schedules vary, so check ahead. Taitung also has large Amis, Puyuma, Paiwan and Bunun communities, and harvest festivals take place in summer. Ask before photographing ceremonies, and choose community-run experiences where you can.

6. A sample 3-day plan

  • Day 1: Train or flight in, pick up the rental car, drive to Chishang for a bike ride through the paddies and a railway bento lunch. Overnight in the Luye or Chishang area.
  • Day 2: Early balloon ride (in season), then drive down the coast with stops at Dulan and Sanxiantai. Overnight in Taitung city or near Zhiben.
  • Day 3: Zhiben hot springs in the morning, a slow walk through Railway Art Village and Tiehua Village, then return the car and head out.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Trying to do Taitung without wheels — buses are too infrequent and the sights are too far apart.
  • Treating it as a day trip from Taipei — the travel time makes anything under two nights feel rushed.
  • Ignoring typhoon forecasts — the east coast takes the worst of them, and roads can close.
  • Driving the coast after dark — it's slow, winding and badly lit in places, so aim to finish before sunset.
