Okinawa for First-Timers: Naha, Beaches, Churaumi Aquarium & Island Hopping

By Adrian Updated October 9, 2026 3 min read

Okinawa is Japan's tropical corner: warm water, island culture and a food scene all its own. Expedia ranked it number two on its 2026 trending-destination list, with search interest up 71 percent. It is also very different from mainland Japan, so plan transport and season carefully.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
Late October to mid-May. Late March to mid-May is the sweet spot for warm weather and calm seas. The rainy season runs roughly May–June, and typhoon season peaks in August and September.
Getting there
Naha Airport (OKA) has direct flights from Tokyo (under 3 hours) and Osaka (about 2 hours). A monorail connects the airport with downtown Naha and Shuri.
Getting around
A rental car is by far the easiest way to reach the north, including the Churaumi Aquarium, but book early and check your license requirements. Naha itself works well on the monorail and on foot.
Minimum time needed
3–5 days for the main island, longer if you add the Kerama Islands or Ishigaki and Miyako.
Rough daily budget
About $50–70/day on a budget, $100–170/day mid-range, not counting flights or a rental car. Trip.com listed Naha hotels from about $35. These are estimates, so confirm current prices.

1. Where to base yourself

  • Naha (Kokusai Street / Asahibashi) — the capital, with the monorail, restaurants and buses. The easiest car-free base.
  • Chatan / American Village — a lively beachside area in the center, with shops and restaurants.
  • Motobu and the north — resorts near the Churaumi Aquarium. Best if you are renting a car and want to be near the sights.

2. Car or no car

Naha and the south are easy without a car. The north is much harder: one guide says it is "extremely difficult" to visit several northern attractions without one. If you rent, drive on the left, reserve well ahead (shortages are common in peak seasons) and check what driving documents your country requires. If not, consider a sightseeing bus tour, a sightseeing taxi or an airport shuttle.

Pro tip Go in late March to mid-May if you can: warm enough for the beach, no typhoon risk, and fewer crowds than July and August. Book your rental car and your flights early for Golden Week.

3. Naha and the south

Walk Kokusai Street and the Makishi Public Market for food. Shuri Castle is still being rebuilt after the 2019 fire, so check what is open before you go. The southern area around Itoman is quick to reach from the airport.

4. The north and the Churaumi Aquarium

The Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium on the Motobu Peninsula is the island's headline sight, with whale sharks and manta rays in one of the world's largest tanks. It is two hours or more from Naha by car depending on traffic, so treat it as a full-day excursion and combine it with the nearby coast.

5. Island hopping

Ferries run from Naha to the Kerama Islands (such as Zamami and Aka) several times a day, with some of the clearest water close to Naha. Further afield, Ishigaki and Miyako are reached by short flights. Check ferry schedules and sea conditions, which can cancel crossings in rough weather.

6. What to eat

  • Goya champuru — bitter-melon stir-fry, the classic Okinawan dish.
  • Okinawa soba and soki soba — wheat noodles with pork, often served with stewed ribs.
  • Taco rice — an Okinawan favorite and a cheap, filling meal.

7. A sample 4-day plan

  • Day 1: Arrive in Naha, Kokusai Street and Makishi Market, soki soba dinner.
  • Day 2: Drive north to the Churaumi Aquarium and the Motobu coast, overnight near Motobu or return to Naha.
  • Day 3: Ferry to Zamami or Aka for snorkeling, or a beach day on the main island.
  • Day 4: Shuri area and a slow lunch before your flight.

8. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Planning a northern day trip without a car — buses are limited and slow.
  • Traveling in August or September without a typhoon plan — flights and ferries can be cancelled.
  • Expecting mainland-Japan prices and seasons — the climate and peak periods are different.
  • Leaving the rental car until the last minute — shortages are common.
