Affiliate Disclosure

Last updated: September 14, 2026

Plyndi is a small, independent site, and affiliate commissions are how it stays online. This page explains exactly how that works, in plain terms.

What this means in practice

Some links on Plyndi — for flights, hotels, activities, tours, travel insurance, or gear — are affiliate links. If you click through and make a booking or purchase, Plyndi may earn a small commission from the booking platform or travel network. This costs you nothing extra; the price you pay is the same whether you use the link or go to the site directly.

Which programs Plyndi participates in

Plyndi works with travel affiliate networks and programs, including (as they're approved and activated):

  • Travelpayouts, an affiliate network representing multiple flight, hotel, and travel-service brands
  • Direct affiliate programs with individual booking platforms, where applicable
  • Display advertising, including Google AdSense, once approved

This list will be kept current as programs are added.

How this affects what gets recommended

It doesn't decide it. A place, hotel type, or activity is included in a guide because it's genuinely useful to know about — not because it pays a higher commission. Where Plyndi hasn't personally verified a specific property or tour, the guide says so rather than implying firsthand experience it doesn't have.

How to tell which links are affiliate links

Any page containing affiliate links carries a visible disclosure banner near the top, like the one on every guide. Affiliate links are also marked with rel="sponsored" in the page code, which is the technical signal search engines use to identify paid or affiliate links.
