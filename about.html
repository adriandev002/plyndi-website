Why Plyndi exists

I kept hitting the same wall planning my own trips. Search “Bangkok guide” or “Bali itinerary” and the top results all read like they were written to rank rather than to help someone standing in an airport working out how to get into the city. Same six stock photos, same vague advice, no actual numbers.

Plyndi is the opposite attempt: guides that give real prices, real transit routes and real trade-offs — including when the honest answer is “skip this one.”

The app came out of the same frustration. Once a trip was planned, it lived across a notes file, a spreadsheet and a pile of booking emails, and the budget fell apart the moment I was paying in two currencies. So I started building the thing I wanted instead.

How a guide gets made

Every destination is researched before it’s published — transit systems, neighbourhood trade-offs, typical costs, and the mistakes first-timers keep making. Guides carry a “last updated” date, and I go back and correct details as prices, hours and closures change rather than leaving stale information live.

I’m not claiming years of professional travel writing. I’m a student doing this alongside coursework. What I can promise: nothing goes up unchecked, corrections get priority over new content, and the site always says plainly when a link is an affiliate link.

The app

Plyndi for iOS holds the itinerary, the trip budget and the packing list in one place, converts expenses at the moment you enter them so a multi-currency trip still adds up, and can draft a first-pass itinerary for you to rearrange. It is not on the App Store yet. The app page lists exactly what’s finished, what’s in progress and what hasn’t been started — I’d rather under-promise on a page you can check than post screenshots of features that don’t exist.

How Plyndi makes money

Affiliate commissions on travel bookings. If you book a stay or a flight through a partner link on a guide, Plyndi may earn a small commission — the price you pay is the same either way. Plyndi is approved with Trip.com and has applied to Booking.com; there is no display advertising on this site. Commissions never decide what a guide recommends. Full details on the affiliate disclosure page.

What’s next

More city guides, added as they’re properly researched rather than rushed out to a schedule, and getting the app through App Store review. If there’s somewhere you want covered, or you’ve spotted something out of date, tell me — reader corrections and suggestions genuinely decide what happens next.
