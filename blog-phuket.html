Phuket for First-Timers: Beaches by Season, Old Town & Island Trips

By Adrian Updated October 9, 2026 3 min read

Phuket is Thailand's biggest island and one of its most-searched destinations, according to Agoda's 2026 data. It does beaches, nightlife, island boat trips and a surprisingly charming old town. The trick is choosing the right coast and the right season.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
November–April is the dry season, with calm seas and open island trips. May–October is the monsoon: red flags on west-coast beaches, some boat trips cancelled, and hotel prices that can drop sharply.
Getting there
Phuket International Airport (HKT) is in the north of the island, with flights from Bangkok and many Asian cities. Allow an hour or more to reach beach areas by taxi, depending on traffic.
Getting around
Taxis and ride-hailing apps, songthaew minibuses, and scooters. Agree taxi fares before you set off, and avoid scooters unless you are an experienced rider with a valid license.
Minimum time needed
4 days for beaches plus one island trip, 5–6 to add Old Town and Phang Nga Bay.
Rough daily budget
About $30–45/day on a budget, $70–120/day mid-range, not counting flights. Trip.com listed hotels from about $15 a night. These are estimates, so confirm current prices.

1. Where to base yourself

  • Patong — the nightlife hub, with bars, clubs and live music. Busy and noisy.
  • Kata and Karon — wide beaches suited to swimming and families, with a more relaxed feel.
  • Kamala and the north-west coast — quieter beaches and resorts, further from the action.
  • Phuket Old Town — Sino-Portuguese shophouses, cafes and galleries, a good base if you are not beach-focused.

2. Beaches, safety and the monsoon

West-coast beaches are lovely in the dry season and dangerous in the monsoon, when strong currents and red flags are common. Always swim between the flags, and never swim where a red flag is flying. If you travel between May and October, expect some rain and rough seas, but also cheaper hotels and fewer crowds. Old Town and Phang Nga Bay trips still work in many conditions.

Pro tip Do the Big Buddha and Wat Chalong in the morning, then spend the afternoon in Old Town. Take any island boat trip on a weekday, when it is quieter and cheaper.

3. Old Town, Big Buddha and the temples

Phuket Old Town is free to walk and best on Sundays, when the Lard Yai walking street runs from about 4pm to 10pm. The Big Buddha on the ridge above Chalong is free, with great views, a strict dress code (sarongs are lent at the gate) and is best before about 11am. Wat Chalong is the island's best-known temple, and Promthep Cape is the classic sunset viewpoint, so arrive early in high season.

4. Island and bay trips

  • Phi Phi Islands — speedboat day trips were listed at roughly ฿2,500–3,000 plus a park fee in 2026. Maya Bay has had seasonal landing closures, so check before you book.
  • Phang Nga Bay — limestone cliffs and sea-canoe tours, roughly ฿1,400–2,800 plus a park fee.
  • Similan Islands — snorkeling and diving, open only in the dry season (roughly mid-October to mid-May).

Prices come from one 2026 listing, so confirm with the operator, check what is included, and avoid the cheapest offers.

5. What to eat

  • Mee Hokkien and Old Town street food — local noodle dishes and snacks unique to Phuket's Hokkien heritage.
  • Fresh seafood — grilled at beach restaurants, often priced by weight, so ask first.
  • Vegetarian Festival — held around October, with street food and processions, if your dates match.

6. A sample 4-day plan

  • Day 1: Arrive, settle in at Kata or Karon, sunset at Promthep Cape.
  • Day 2: Big Buddha and Wat Chalong in the morning, Old Town in the afternoon, Sunday walking street if timing fits.
  • Day 3: Phi Phi or Phang Nga Bay boat trip.
  • Day 4: Beach morning, a slow lunch, and a last seafood dinner.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Swimming under a red flag — rip currents in the monsoon season are serious.
  • Booking one hotel without checking the season — Patong is lively, Kata is calmer, Old Town is not a beach base.
  • Renting a scooter without experience — many tourists are injured every year.
  • Paying the first taxi price — agree the fare or use an app.
