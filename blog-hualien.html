Hualien & Taroko Gorge: Marble Canyons, Hiking & the East Coast Rail

By Adrian Updated October 8, 2026 3 min read

Taroko Gorge is the single best natural sight on Taiwan's main island: a marble canyon carved by a river, with tunnels, suspension bridges and waterfalls. It's close to Hualien city, which is a relaxed base with a great night market. The catch is that conditions change, so check what's open first.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
March–May and October–November for comfortable hiking weather. Summer brings heavy rain and typhoon risk, and winter can be cold and wet.
Getting there
Taroko or Puyuma Express from Taipei takes roughly 2–2.5 hours. Reserve in advance, because weekends and holidays sell out.
Getting around
Taxis and chartered day tours are the most flexible way to see the gorge. Check current tourist shuttle options from Hualien and Xincheng stations.
Minimum time needed
2 days: one for the gorge and one for Hualien city and the coast.
Rough daily budget
$40–70/day mid-range, plus a taxi or guided day tour for the gorge. Check current prices.

1. First: check what's open

After the major earthquake in April 2024, sections of Taroko National Park and some trails were closed for long periods, and status has kept changing since. Before you plan the day, check the national park's official website for current trail and road conditions. Some routes, such as the Zhuilu Old Trail, need an advance permit and have daily quotas. Never assume a trail you've seen in old photos is open.

2. Where to base yourself

Hualien city is the standard base: it has the train station, plenty of hotels and the Dongdamen Night Market, a large nightly market with a section of Indigenous food stalls. If your priority is early access to the gorge, Xincheng near Taroko station puts you closer to the entrance.

Pro tip Go to the gorge early. Tour buses typically arrive from mid-morning, and the narrow sections get very crowded. A weekday start before 9am gives you the best light and the quietest trails.

3. Taroko Gorge highlights

  • Shakadang Trail — a gentle riverside walk, good for most fitness levels.
  • Swallow Grotto — sheer marble cliffs pocked with holes, a short walk with big views.
  • Tunnel of Nine Turns — the narrowest, most dramatic stretch of the canyon.
  • Changchun Shrine — a small shrine below a waterfall, a good photo stop.
  • Baiyang Trail — tunnels and a waterfall cave; bring a flashlight and a hard hat if required.

Wear a hard hat where signs tell you to, since rockfall is real. Carry water and rain gear.

4. Beyond the gorge

Qixingtan Beach is a pebble beach on the Pacific, good for an easy sunrise or sunset. Liyu Lake is a calm lake with a bike loop. In the rift valley south of the city, the daylily fields at Sixty Stone Mountain and Chike Mountain bloom orange around August and September.

5. What to eat

Hualien is known for wonton soup, steamed buns and chewy mochi. At Dongdamen look for the Indigenous-style stalls, which are one of the more distinctive eating experiences on the island. Prices are modest and portions are generous.

6. A sample 2-day plan

  • Day 1: Morning train from Taipei, check the park's website for open trails, then a late-afternoon walk at Qixingtan Beach and dinner at Dongdamen Night Market.
  • Day 2: Leave early for Taroko Gorge: Shakadang Trail, Swallow Grotto and the Tunnel of Nine Turns, with any other open trails after lunch. Return to Hualien for an evening train.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Arriving without checking closures — conditions change often, so confirm what's open the day before.
  • Cramming Taroko into a half-day — the gorge rewards a full day with short walks and no rush.
  • Not reserving the train — Taroko Express seats sell out on weekends and holidays.
  • Skipping rain gear and a hat — the gorge is humid and the weather turns fast.
