Bali on a Budget: Rice Fields, Waterfalls & Warungs

By Axel Updated September 14, 2026 10 min read

Bali's reputation runs on two extremes online — infinity pools tagged to death on Instagram, or warnings that it's "too touristy now." Neither is the full picture. Pick the right base and it's still genuinely cheap and genuinely beautiful; you just have to know where the markup lives.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
April–October (dry season). July–August is peak crowds and price.
Getting around
Scooter rental if you're comfortable riding (helmet always, international license technically required); Grab/Gojek for everything else.
Minimum time needed
7 days to cover Canggu, Ubud, and Uluwatu without rushing.
Rough daily budget
$25–35/day backpacker, $50–90/day mid-range, not counting flights.

1. Pick your base — you can't do it all from one town

Bali is bigger than it looks on a map, and traffic between regions is slow. Splitting your stay across two or three bases beats trying to day-trip everywhere from one hotel.

  • Canggu — surf, beach clubs, the best co-working/cafe scene, flattest area to scooter around. Best base for your first few days to adjust.
  • Ubud — inland, cooler, rice terraces and jungle on your doorstep, the "cultural" base. No real beach nearby, so pair it with a coastal stop.
  • Uluwatu — clifftop, dramatic coastline, best surf breaks, quieter than Canggu but pricier food since it's more resort-driven.

A common, well-paced first trip: 3 nights Canggu → 2 nights Ubud → 2 nights Uluwatu, in that order so you end on the coast.

2. Ubud's rice terraces without the crowd shot everyone has

Tegalalang Rice Terrace is the famous one and it's genuinely striking, but it's also the most crowded and has the most "donation" gates and photo-prop hawkers per square meter. Two alternatives with the same terraced-hillside views and a fraction of the people:

  • Tegallalang's quieter neighbor terraces a short walk further along the same road, past the main viewing platforms.
  • Jatiluwih Rice Terraces — about an hour further out, a UNESCO-recognized site, much bigger and far less visited.

If you only have time for one, go at sunrise to Tegalalang — the light is better and the tour buses haven't arrived yet.

Pro tip Skip the Ubud Monkey Forest if you're short on time and have seen wildlife elsewhere on your trip — it's fine, not essential, and the macaques there are habituated enough to grab sunglasses and snacks off visitors.

3. Waterfalls actually worth the hike

Bali has dozens of waterfalls listed on maps; a lot are a muddy 20-minute walk for a small trickle. These two consistently deliver:

  • Tibumana Waterfall — near Ubud, an easy walk in, genuinely swimmable pool, less crowded than the famous Tegenungan.
  • Sekumpul Waterfall — in the north, a proper multi-tier waterfall and a longer trek (guide recommended, arranged locally), but the most dramatic in Bali by a wide margin.

4. Uluwatu: temple, sunset, and the Kecak fire dance

Uluwatu Temple sits on a cliff edge with genuinely dramatic ocean views. Time your visit for late afternoon and stay for the Kecak fire dance at sunset — a traditional Balinese performance with a chanting circle of performers, no instruments, staged right at the cliff with the sunset behind it. Buy tickets on arrival; it's inexpensive and books up on weekends.

Watch your belongings and don't wear dangly earrings or hold food near the temple monkeys — they're notorious for snatching sunglasses and anything shiny.

5. Warungs over restaurants (most of the time)

A "warung" is a small, family-run local eatery, and it's where the best value and often the best food is. A full plate of nasi campur (rice with a selection of sides) at a local warung costs a fraction of the same dish at a restaurant aimed at tourists, and it's usually better. Save restaurant budget for one or two nicer sunset dinners in Canggu or Uluwatu, and eat warung-style the rest of the trip.

If you want to actually learn the food rather than just eat it, a half-day Balinese cooking class in Ubud (many include a market visit) is one of the better-value activity bookings on the island.

6. A day trip worth the boat ride

Nusa Penida, a short fast-boat ride from Sanur, has the cliff-and-turquoise-water views that circulate endlessly online (Kelingking Beach, Broken Beach, Angel's Billabong). It's a long day — early boat out, late boat back — and the island's roads are rough, so book a driver rather than self-riding a scooter unless you're very confident.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Renting a scooter with zero experience — Bali's roads and traffic patterns are unforgiving for beginners; take a car/driver instead if you're not confident.
  • Not checking visa-on-arrival requirements before flying — rules vary by nationality and length of stay, so verify against your own passport before booking.
  • Skipping travel insurance that covers scooter accidents — a genuinely common source of trip-ending injuries and expensive medical bills for visitors.
  • Drinking tap water — stick to bottled or filtered water, including for brushing teeth.
