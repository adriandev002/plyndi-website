Kaohsiung for First-Timers: Harbor Art, Night Markets & Cijin Island

By Adrian Updated October 8, 2026 3 min read

Kaohsiung is Taiwan's big southern port city, and most visitors rush straight past it on the way to Kenting. That's a mistake. It's warmer, more relaxed and less crowded than Taipei, the old harbor has been turned into a genuinely good art district, and the food is cheap.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
November–March: dry, sunny and comfortable. Summers are hot and humid, and typhoon season runs roughly July–October.
Getting there
High-speed rail (HSR) to Zuoying station takes about 1.5–2 hours from Taipei. One-way fares are roughly NT$1,500, so check current prices and book a reserved seat on weekends.
Getting around
A two-line MRT (Red and Orange), the Circular Light Rail, and ferries. An EasyCard works on all of them.
Minimum time needed
2 full days for the city, 3 if you add Fo Guang Shan or a slow day on Cijin Island.
Rough daily budget
$30–45/day budget, $60–90/day mid-range, not counting the train. Hotels tend to be cheaper than Taipei. Prices here are estimates, so confirm current fares and opening hours before you book.

1. Where to base yourself

The HSR station (Zuoying) sits in the north of the city, but the MRT Red Line connects it to downtown in about 15 minutes, so you don't need to stay near it.

  • Formosa Boulevard — the Red and Orange line interchange, the most central base for MRT access, nightlife and Liuhe Night Market.
  • Yancheng / Pier-2 area — quieter, creative, close to the harbor art district and cafes, with the Light Rail running past.
  • Near the Love River — a pleasant evening walk and reasonable hotels, a little less convenient for the northern sights.

2. Harbor art and the Dome of Light

The Pier-2 Art Center is a cluster of old harbor warehouses turned into galleries, outdoor sculpture, small shops and cafes. It's best in the late afternoon, when the light is soft and the heat has dropped, and it pairs naturally with a walk along the nearby railway park and the Hamasen area.

At Formosa Boulevard station look up in the main concourse: the Dome of Light is a huge stained-glass ceiling and one of the largest glass artworks in the world. It costs nothing to see because it's inside a station you'll probably pass through anyway.

Pro tip Take the ferry to Cijin Island around 4pm, watch the sunset from the western coast, eat seafood for dinner, and take the ferry back after dark. It's cooler, quieter and a better light than a midday visit.

3. Lotus Pond and the temples

On the northern side of the city, Lotus Pond has a cluster of colorful temples and pagodas. The Dragon and Tiger Pagodas are the famous pair: the local custom is to enter through the dragon's mouth and leave through the tiger's mouth for good luck. Take the Red Line to Zuoying and finish the last stretch by bus, taxi or bike. The whole area is easy to cover in about two hours. Dress modestly at active temples.

4. Fo Guang Shan Buddha Memorial Center

This is a large Buddhist complex in Dashu District, about an hour out of the city, with a giant seated Buddha, a museum and gardens. Entry is free. It works as a half-day trip, and many visitors eat in the vegetarian dining hall. Check current bus options from Zuoying station before you go, because routes and frequency change. Wear shoulders-and-knees-covered clothing and keep your voice down in the halls.

5. Cijin Island by ferry

Cijin Island is a thin strip of land across the harbor from downtown. Take the short ferry from the Gushan ferry terminal and rent a bike on arrival. The island has a black-sand beach, a lighthouse, wind turbines, and a street lined with fresh seafood and snacks. Go on a weekday if you can; on weekends the ferry queues and the old street get crowded.

6. Night markets worth your evening

  • Liuhe Night Market — the best known and the most tourist-oriented, right by Formosa Boulevard. It's open nightly and easy to reach, and papaya milk and seafood are the usual order.
  • Ruifeng Night Market — more locals and fewer foreigners, but it doesn't operate every night, so check which nights it's open before you head out.

7. A sample 3-day plan

  • Day 1: Arrive by HSR, drop your bags, see the Dome of Light at Formosa Boulevard, then late-afternoon Pier-2 and the harbor. Dinner at Liuhe Night Market.
  • Day 2: Morning at Lotus Pond and the Dragon and Tiger Pagodas. Afternoon ferry to Cijin Island, sunset on the coast, seafood dinner, ferry back.
  • Day 3: Half-day at Fo Guang Shan, then back to the HSR station for your onward train to Kenting, Tainan or Taipei.

8. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Sightseeing in the midday sun between May and September — plan indoor stops (Dome of Light, museums) for noon and walk outdoors early or late.
  • Treating Kaohsiung as a transit stop only — one night on the way to Kenting skips the best parts of the city.
  • Taking taxis everywhere — the MRT and Light Rail cover most of what visitors want, and use a card instead of cash.
  • Visiting only Liuhe Night Market — it's convenient but touristy, so add a more local market if your schedule allows.
