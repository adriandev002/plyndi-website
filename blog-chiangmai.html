Chiang Mai: Temples, Ethical Elephant Sanctuaries & the Pai Road Trip

By Adrian Updated September 14, 2026 10 min read

Where Bangkok is fast and dense, Chiang Mai is slow and green — a walkable old city ringed by a moat, mountains visible from most streets, and a pace that makes it the default second stop for people who liked Bangkok but wanted quiet.

Quick facts

Best time to visit
November–February (cool, clear). Avoid March–April: seasonal agricultural burning causes heavy smoke haze.
Getting around
Old City is walkable; a rented scooter or Grab covers everything else.
Minimum time needed
3 full days, 5 with a Pai trip.
Rough daily budget
$20–30/day backpacker, $45–75/day mid-range — one of the cheaper bases in Southeast Asia.

1. The Old City temple walk

Chiang Mai's Old City is a walled, moated square packed with temples, and unlike Bangkok's, most are free to enter and rarely crowded. A single afternoon on foot covers the highlights:

  • Wat Phra Singh — the most revered temple in the city, elaborate Lanna-style architecture.
  • Wat Chedi Luang — a massive, partially ruined 14th-century chedi, one of the most atmospheric sites in the city, especially late afternoon.
  • Wat Phan Tao — a smaller wooden teak temple next to Chedi Luang, easy to miss and worth not missing.

2. Doi Suthep: worth the trip up the mountain

Wat Phra That Doi Suthep sits on a mountain overlooking the city, reached by a short songthaew (shared truck-taxi) ride and a 300-step naga staircase (or a funicular, if you'd rather skip the stairs). Go for sunset if you can — the golden chedi against the darkening valley view below is the single best photo opportunity in the region.

Pro tip Songthaews to Doi Suthep leave from near Chiang Mai Gate once there are enough passengers — negotiate the round-trip fare including wait time before you get in, not after.

3. Elephant sanctuaries: how to actually vet one

Chiang Mai's elephant tourism industry has shifted hard toward "ethical sanctuaries" as a marketing term, and not all of them deserve it. The distinction that actually matters: does the visit involve riding, or performances (painting, football, etc.)? Both indicate the animals were broken through harsh training methods to tolerate it. A genuine sanctuary lets you observe, feed, and sometimes bathe elephants on their terms, with no riding and no tricks.

  • Read recent reviews specifically mentioning "no riding" and "no chains" rather than trusting the name alone.
  • Smaller herd sizes and visible space to roam are good signs.
  • Expect to pay more for a genuine sanctuary than a riding camp — it's a real cost difference, not just marketing.

4. Sunday Walking Street

Every Sunday evening, Ratchadamnoen Road through the Old City closes to traffic and becomes a market stretching over a kilometer — textiles, local art, and food stalls filling the temple courtyards along the route. It's genuinely one of the better night markets in Thailand and worth planning a Sunday into your trip for.

5. Nimman: coffee and the digital-nomad district

West of the Old City, Nimmanhaemin Road (Nimman) is Chiang Mai's cafe and co-working hub — a good half-day if you want strong coffee, boutique shopping, and a break from temple-hopping. It's also where a lot of the city's longer-term expat and remote-work community bases itself.

6. Pai: the road trip everyone talks about

Pai is a small mountain town about 3–4 hours from Chiang Mai by a famously winding road (often quoted at over 700 curves). It's doable as a long day trip by minivan but genuinely better as a 1–2 night stop if your schedule allows — waterfalls, hot springs, and a laid-back backpacker town that feels worlds away from Chiang Mai's pace. If you get motion sick easily, take motion sickness medication before the drive or book a private car instead of a shared minivan.

7. Common first-timer mistakes

  • Booking a riding-elephant "sanctuary" without checking recent reviews — the ethical-sounding name isn't a guarantee.
  • Visiting during burning season (Mar–Apr) without checking air quality first — it can be genuinely bad for respiratory health some years.
  • Renting a scooter without checking your travel insurance covers it — many policies exclude scooter accidents without a proper motorcycle license.
  • Doing Pai as a rushed day trip when you had the extra night to spare — the road alone eats half the day.
